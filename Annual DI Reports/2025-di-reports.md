### ✅ Annual Website Audits

In 2025, I did some updating to the VEA website, per usual. A lot was focused on SEO, but I did intentionally looked at representation as part of my new image choices for the site.

Additionally, I did achieve my goal of making more time and energy available to focus on telling the stories of underrepresented folks more in-depth. This mainly came in the form of blog posts. I was much better about this earlier in the year (had a hard time keeping up with blogging after busy season started). But of the four new event feature blog posts I did in 2025, three of them featured LGBTQIA+ couples.

I intend to continue these efforts in 2026. A specific goal I have is to feature at least two more couples belonging to underrepresented groups on my blog. I also will be going through my site and updating imagery, with an even larger representation of LGBTQIA+ folks as a priority.

### ✅ Social Media Audits

I still don’t have a consistent formula for posting to social media, and this is somewhat intentional. I showed up on social a lot less in general in 2025 for the sake of my mental health, and also prioritizing time and energy in more meaningful areas (e.g. blogging client stories, connecting with clients and peers more directly via email and meet-ups, contributing to my community in meaningful ways, etc). I did check in on my socials, per usual (though not in a formal, scheduled manner) to assess what content I was sharing, who was represented in it, how often, and where. Inclusive language remained a priority, and was consistently used. Something new this year that I tried to be much better about was mentioning my clients’ pronouns in each post. This is something I honestly can’t believe I wasn’t doing consistently before, but will remain a priority in 2026.

I also think I did a better job this past year with representation on my social media feed. It helps that I had the joy of working with a lot more LGBTQIA+ folks this past year than the previous year. But even in years where, for whatever reason, that doesn’t occur, I do want to have a plan and method for ensuring representation is something that appears in my feed regularly.

That all said, I do plan to continue to disengage from social media in general a bit this year. I need to do this for my own well-being, but I also believe it’s not truly the most valuable way for me to show up for and engage with my clients. Many of my clients don’t even have social media nowadays! Instead, I want to focus on using social media to amplify other important work I’m doing (e.g. blog posts, sharing community resources, etc) and yes, still sharing imagery from client events as well, when appropriate. However, I aim to make my time in social media more intentional and rationed.

She completed the D+I questionnaire previously, and also signed a new contract as an associate that included a D+I clause. ### ✅ Contract Clause

All VEA contracts (wedding, event, portrait, second shooter, etc) still include a clause which states:

_In the event that Photographer identifies any inappropriate, threatening, hostile, racist, homophobic, transphobic, xenophobic, misogynistic, or otherwise hateful or offensive behavior directed at them or their employees, contractors, agents, or other, Photographer reserves the right to end photography, retain all fees paid by the Client, and hold no further responsibilities to the Client._

_Photographer agrees to notify the Client of any of the above listed behaviors, and give them an opportunity to correct such behaviors, or remove the offending individuals from the session prior to ending photography_

### ✅ Vet All Directory Advertising Organizations to Which I Give my Money to Ensure D+I Policies are in Place

There were only two directories that I paid to be present in in 2025, and they were Wandering Weddings and Equally Wed. Wandering Weddings still could improve on their visibility of D+I, and don’t seem to have any “policies” in place, but they do have an explicit statement about their “Commitment to Equality” on their website. Equally Wed is specifically geared towards underrepresented groups, and D+I is at the forefront of their product.

I absolutely need to maintain my listing with Wandering Weddings this year, as that has been a main source of inquiries and bookings already for this year. That said, Equally Wed has not been a source of inquiries or bookings for many years, and I think I need to reassess paying to advertise there, just from a financial standpoint.

### ✅ Maintain a Presence in at Least 2 Directories that are Specifically Focused on Reaching Underrepresented Marriers

I met this goal again this year - I maintained a presence on both Equally Wed and Everywhere is Queer (EIQ). The former is a paid listing, and the latter is free.

In 2026, I’m thinking about dropping my paid listing with Equally Wed, as it hasn’t had a good ROI from an inquiries/bookings standpoint for quite some time. I will maintain my free listing with EIQ, and if Equally Wed allows for a free listing, I’ll do that too. If they do not, I’ll need to investigate finding other locations that focus on reaching underrepresented groups in the wedding industry.

### ✅ Giving Back Program

I maintained my Giving Back Program in 2025 (for every wedding or elopement booking that Venture Ever After receives, I will donate a set amount of money (as of 2025: $200) to a select organization doing work in human rights, environmental issues, or both). The total donated for 2025 was $3000!

I also spent some time assessing the organizations that receive donations from the Giving Back Program. I made some huge changes, including adding in three more organizations: Washington Trails Association, Friends of Outdoor School, and Pride Northwest. I also shifted how donations are dispersed; booking clients now recommend where I send $100, and then I select where to send the other $100. This was done in an effort to ensure that ALL the organizations on my donations list receive funds from Venture Ever After each year (previously, some organizations were receiving a disproportionate amount of donations compared to others). I’m really excited to say that this has been working splendidly and I’ve been able to donate to all the organizations on the list in 2025, and am very excited to do so again in 2026.

## Summary

In 2025, I achieved 100% of my D+I goals. This is the first year that has happened! I’m really excited about this, and I think it means I finally reached a balance of challenging myself to continue to work harder and more meaningfully on these goals, while also ensuring they really are achievable.

I do think that some of the existing goals are so engrained in my business practices, that they will likely automatically happen each year. However, I don’t think that’s a bad thing. I want to keep them on this list of goals as a way to continually remind myself that they’re important, and to continue to track them and hold myself accountable to them.

I also know that some of these goals were very close to not being achieved (mainly those around networking, continuing education, and how I show up digitally). Knowing this, these will be key areas for me to focus on in 2026. I think these goals will continue to challenge me, and I’m excited to see what I learn from them this year and how they will affect my goal-setting for 2027.
